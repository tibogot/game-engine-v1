Props & movers: pick one above and a ghost follows your mouse — LMB to place (keeps going, so you can lay a run of cones), Esc or RMB to put it down. The Snap button picks the surface it rides: Ground = terrain, Road = decks only (ghost turns red off the road), Auto = whichever you are pointing at. Placed something already? Right-click it to select and fine-tune with the gizmo.

Road pieces — point at the track. The next piece follows your mouse, just like a prop ghost: move the cursor near any open end and the piece jumps there, switching chains for you. LMB/Enter places it. Point at nothing and the piece stays exactly where it is, so a piece you positioned by hand is never dragged off by a stray mouse move.
The floating arrows are the open ends, and each points the way a piece placed there would grow: blue = a chain's end (keep going), purple = a chain's start (builds backwards), gold = a junction exit (starts a side road). The big white one is where the piece will actually land if you click now.

The gizmo is for empty space. Use it when you want a piece somewhere there is no track yet — drag to move it anywhere (Y axis = height), and it still magnets onto an open end if you drop it close enough. W move Shift+E rotate • Q/E yaw nudge • N new chain • [/] select chain (gizmo then drags the whole chain).

Either end: a chain grows from its tail or its head — press O (or drag the ghost onto the far end) to switch, and the status line always says which one you are on. Building backwards prepends: the piece goes in front and the chain's start moves back to meet it, so nothing already placed moves — you can extend a finished run backwards to a new start line without breaking a single seam you had aligned. A start that is already joined to a junction branch or to another chain is not an open end, so O will decline there and say so.

Junctions — one arm continues by itself. A fork, T, crossroads or roundabout is an ordinary chain piece: the chain drives in one end and out the far side, so you do not have to do anything to keep going on the main arm — just place the next piece.
The EXTRA exits are the floating yellow arrows. Point at one (or press K) and place, and the side road starts as its own chain; the arrow disappears once something is built on it. A crossroads and a roundabout have two arrows — K steps through them one press at a time, and pointing at one goes straight to it. R flips which side a Split / T uses and which arm of a Y fork the chain follows. A roundabout's island is a hole — cut across the middle and you drop through it.

Splitting and rejoining: build the alternate route off a Split's arrow, run it where you like, then press J (Join) to close the gap back into a Merge. A chain is a rigid run of pieces, so nothing you place by hand will ever land on both ends at once — J generates a link shaped to whatever is left over, meeting the far end exactly in position and heading. It tells you the gap it closed and the tightest radius, and refuses outright if the only curve that would fit is a hairpin — build a little further out and try again.

Undo: Backspace or Ctrl+Z steps back through every edit — placing, deleting, replacing, tilting, dragging, even Clear. Shift+Backspace, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes; right-click the Undo button for the same. The shortcuts follow the letter PRINTED on your key, so Ctrl+Z is Ctrl+Z on AZERTY too — and Backspace works on any layout at all. A gizmo drag is one step, not one per frame; loading a track starts a fresh history.
